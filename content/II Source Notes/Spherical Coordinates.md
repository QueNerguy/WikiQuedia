### Spherical Coordinates
20-05-2025
#### Referencing:
[Wikipedia - Spherical coordinate system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_coordinate_system)

#### Synopsis:
Where cartesian coordinates define a point in space as a function of the distance in the direction of two or more axes, at a right angle of eachother, _spherical coordinates_ define a point in space as:
1. A radial distance ($r$) from the origin (also known as the pole).
2. An angle ($\theta$) with the _polar axis_, a line, typically drawn to point __upward__ from the pole.
3. An angle ($\phi$) around the _polar axis_.
This means that $\phi$ works in what would be the $x, y$ plane in cartesian coordinates, and that $\theta$ works around what would be the $z$-axis in cartesian coordinates. <br>Spherical coordinates are chosen __only for 3 dimensions__. <br><br>A coordinate system that uses _angles_ and a _radial distance_ for 2 dimensions, is called the [polar coordinate system](Polar%20Coordinates).<br><br><br>![[Kugelkoord_funnsyphereSphericalCoordinates_80%.png|450]]<br>By Ag2gaeh - Own work, CC BY-SA 4.0, [link](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=134565435)<br><br>
