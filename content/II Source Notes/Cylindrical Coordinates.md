### Cylindrical Coordinates
20-05-2025
#### Referencing:
1. Wikipedia, *Cylindrical coordinate system*, [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylindrical_coordinate_system).

#### Synopsis:
Where cartesian coordinates define a point in space as a function of the distance in the direction of two or more axes, at a right angle of eachother, _cylindrical coordinates_ define a point in space as:
1. A radial distance ($r$) from the origin.
2. An angle ($\theta$ or $\phi$) with the _polar axis_, a line, typically drawn to point __to the right__ of the origin.
3. A height ($z$) above the origin. This is equivalent to the $z$-axis in cartesian coordinates.

Cylindrical coordinates are useful for working with circular and tube-shaped objects in 3 dimensions.<br>Cylindrical coordinates are chosen __only for 3 dimensions__. <br><br>A coordinate system that uses _angles_ and a _radial distance_ for 2 dimensions, is called the [polar coordinate system](Polar%20Coordinates).
