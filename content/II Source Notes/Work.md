#### Synopsis:
Work, $W$, is the amount of energy generated by a force, as it is exerted over a distance. When pushing a cart, you do work over the distance that you move the cart. If you stop pushing the cart and it rolls down a hill because of gravity, you stop doing work. Instead, gravity starts doing work. You have only done work only over the distance that you pushed it. <br>If a force doesn't point in the exact direction of travel, only the component in the direction of movement does work. If you were to push the cart slightly sideways, you only do work for the part of the force that actually makes the cart move along it's path, not the part that tries to move it sideways.
$$
W = F \cdot s
$$
or in the case of a generalized non-straight path, where $\textbf{F}$ may be a function of the path $s$, we have
$$
W=\int\vec{F}\cdot d \vec{s}
$$
$$
\delta W = \vec{F} d\vec{s}
$$
As work is a measure of __change__ in energy, it is a property that is not inherent to a certain state. Instead, it only plays a role when comparing two states, where work would be exchanged to get from state $a$ to state $b$. It is therefore not a _state variable_, but instead a _path variable_, a variable that is dependent on the _path the system took_ to change from state $a$ to state $b$. <br>The inexact differential $\delta$ denotes that work is _path variable_. <br>Pushing a cart in a circle would require work all the way, thus our exerted work would only increase and never go to 0. This is exactly how a path variable is defined. Thus, when integrating the work done by the system over a full cycle it's value does not return to 0 like what would be the case for state variables. 
$$
\oint \delta W = \oint \vec{F} d \vec{s} \ne 0
$$
##### Rate of Work
Saying the rate of work is a different way of saying the amount of work done over a certain time period. This measure is also known as [power](Power) $P$. The unit is then also $[J/s]$ or $[W]$.
$$
\dot{W} = P

##### [In Thermodynamics](Work%20in%20Thermodynamics)
In thermodynamics work is produced by changing the size of the system, thus work is only done when the volume of the system changes, like a piston in a combustion engine
- Work is **positive** when it is **done by** the system, as it flows **out of** the system.

To use [pressure](Pressure) instead of force in the mathematical definition of work we have
$$
W= \int p \cdot A \cdot ds
$$
$$
W=\int p \cdot dV \Rightarrow  p \Delta V
$$
$$
\delta W = p dV
$$
Where $p$ is pressure, and $V$ is the volume of the system. The inexact differential $\delta$ denotes that the work is _path variable_, not a state variable. <br>For the rate of work $\dot{W}$ we have
$$
\dot{W} = \frac{\delta W}{dt} = \frac{W}{t}
$$
$$
\dot{W} = \frac{p dV}{dt} = \frac{\int p dV}{t}
$$
